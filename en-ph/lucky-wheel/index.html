How the Lucky Wheel triggers

TaDa's product page is specific: land the wheel symbol in the center of the special reel. That's the trigger, full stop. You're not collecting three scatters across the 3×3 to open a free-spins round — this is a different mechanism entirely.

Prize ladder, as TaDa states it

Cash prizes as multiples of base wager: 1×, 3×, 5×, 8×, 10×, 15×, 20×, 30×, 50×, 100×, 200×, 1,000×. The top step is large precisely because a working ladder needs a lot of small stops underneath it.

BandSteps on the ladderWhat it actually means

Keep the special-reel multipliers (1×–15×) separate from this ladder in your head. A 15× on the reel and a 15× on the wheel are different events — don't merge them when reviewing a session.

What Extra Bet changes here

With Extra Bet active, TaDa describes an upgraded wheel plus an extra random multiplier on wheel wins, drawn from the 1×–15× set. You're paying +50% stake for that package. Read features before leaving Extra Bet switched on by default.

About the word "Scatter" on JILI's blurb

JILI's short marketing text uses Scatter language when describing improved Lucky Wheel odds. We still describe the trigger the way TaDa's mechanical copy does — a wheel symbol on the special reel — rather than inventing a free-spins chapter out of one marketing word.

What a first-time visitor should expect

Long stretches without a wheel are normal. A wheel that lands on a small multiple is normal. A highlight clip is a marketing object, not a schedule. The card max win stays 10,000× as the lead ceiling figure — see RTP for the 15,025× conflict.

If a session only feels "real" once the wheel opens, you'll over-spin waiting for it. Pair this page with how to play before judging the game on wheel frequency alone.

Lucky Wheel and bankroll psychology

The 1,000× top step is vivid. The small steps are what you'll actually see most, if the wheel opens at all. Building a session around the top step is how people skip their own stop-loss. Set a hard cap on Extra Bet spins you'll run specifically to chase the wheel — then stop even if it never shows.
